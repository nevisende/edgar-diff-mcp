## Prompt

Diff Exxon's risk factors between its last two 10-Ks.

## Answer

I compared Exxon Mobil Corp.’s two latest 10-Ks:

- Newer: year ended December 31, 2025; filed February 18, 2026; accession `0000034088-26-000045` ([filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm)).
- Older: year ended December 31, 2024; filed February 19, 2025; accession `0000034088-25-000010` ([filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408825000010/xom-20241231.htm)).

Item 1A remained broadly similar: 45 versus 47 paragraphs, with 32 changed, 13 unchanged, no newly added standalone paragraphs, two removed diff entries, and 86.6% similarity. The two “removed” entries largely reflect paragraph consolidation rather than deletion of whole risks.

### Material changes

1. **Greater emphasis on geopolitical disruption and newly accessible supply**

The newer filing expands economic risks to include armed hostilities, geopolitical volatility, realignment of trade or military alliances, supply-chain networks, and changing shipping routes. It also adds the possibility that government action or world events could open sanctioned, protected, or previously inaccessible resources:

> “access to previously unavailable, sanctioned, or protected oil and gas resources or the availability or opening of new shipping routes.”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `5`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

Exxon further warns that such events can make opportunities available, less available, or unavailable and adversely affect results.

2. **AI appears in several risk contexts**

AI is now identified as:

- A potential source of energy demand through data-center construction:

> “increased demand for artificial intelligence (AI), including the construction and expansion of AI data centers”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `4`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

- A technology Exxon must adopt and integrate into business and decision-making.
- A tool that may strengthen competitors.
- Part of the low-carbon opportunity set through “low-carbon data centers.”

3. **Regulatory and litigation risks are broader and more pointed**

The introductory regulatory-risk language now includes:

> “concerted efforts to increase our legal exposure by groups opposed to the products we provide”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `12`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

It also adds extraterritorial laws, changing government priorities, shifting regulations, and actions that could disrupt energy reliability.

Disclosure mandates may now:

> “require us to make statements we disagree with”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `21`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

Government-action risks now expressly include unwarranted penalties and compelled changes to production plans. Litigation language adds class actions, arbitrations, non-economic damages, government investigations, and jurisdictions adopting liability schemes covering Exxon’s products or operations.

4. **Energy-transition language is more conditional**

The older filing described playing a leading role as the Company’s “objective”:

> “The Company’s objective to play a leading role in the energy transition”  
> (accession `0000034088-25-000010`, Item `1A`, paragraph `27`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408825000010/xom-20241231.htm))

The newer filing says the Company “seeks opportunities” to play that role and adds:

> “Without supportive policies and the innovations they drive, net zero will remain out of reach – for society and for ExxonMobil.”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `27`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

It also states:

> “Society’s progress continues to lag in these areas.”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `27`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

Elsewhere, “any successful energy transition” is reframed as:

> “meeting society's needs for energy and reducing emissions”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `30`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

5. **More expansive greenhouse-gas policy risk**

The newer filing adds mandates requiring disclosure of emission-reduction plans or reductions in the use or production of certain products. It also broadens the actors named from political actors to political actors and non-governmental organizations, and extends the compliance-cost warning to current, pending, and potential policies.

6. **Low-carbon opportunity descriptions were updated**

The newer filing expands or refines the listed businesses and technologies to include hydrogen and ammonia, Proxxima resin systems, and low-carbon data centers. References to succeeding “through the energy transition” were changed to succeeding “in a lower-emissions future.”

7. **Pioneer-specific integration language was removed**

The older filing specifically mentioned:

> “including the acquisition of Pioneer.”  
> (accession `0000034088-25-000010`, Item `1A`, paragraph `38`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408825000010/xom-20241231.htm))

The newer filing retains the general risk of successfully integrating acquisitions and achieving anticipated synergies, but no longer names Pioneer.  
(accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `36`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

8. **Reputation risk now recognizes conflicting transition expectations**

The newer filing adds:

> “divergent and evolving societal views and investor pressures regarding a future energy transition”  
> (accession `0000034088-26-000045`, Item `1A`, paragraph `43`, [url](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/34088/000003408826000045/xom-20251231.htm))

Overall, the newer Item 1A preserves Exxon’s core commodity-price, operational, governmental, climate-policy, cybersecurity, and competition risks, while materially sharpening its treatment of geopolitical realignment, contested regulation and litigation, AI, energy reliability, and the dependence of net-zero ambitions on policy and technological support.
